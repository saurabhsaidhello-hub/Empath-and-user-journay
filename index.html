Empathy Mapping & User Journeys

In Their
Shoes

Imagine you're ordering lunch on an app. Your class starts in 10 minutes, and the Pay button doesn't work. You feel stressed. The person who built the app never felt that, because they never used it in your situation.

Designers use two simple tools to understand people like you:

An empathy map is one page about one person. It shows what they say, think, do and feel.

A user journey map follows that person step by step while they finish one task, like ordering lunch.

In this guide, our example is Maya, a student with 12 minutes between classes, ordering lunch on an app called Campus Eats.

( What is it )

Empathy mapping is a way to characterise your target users in order to make effective design decisions.

An empathy map is a square split into four parts around one person. The gaps between the parts are where the insights hide.

If your research notes are a pile of bricks, the empathy map is the first wall. It sorts what you learned about a person into four questions, so the team sees a human being instead of a spreadsheet.

Left side you can see and hear. Right side you must infer
( Four quadrants )
01
Says
Direct quotes from interviews. Use the person's own words, including complaints and praise.
02
Thinks
What goes on in their head: worries they keep quiet, beliefs and expectations. You infer this from what they say and do.
03
Does
Behaviour you can observe: habits, actions and workarounds. Often this differs from what people say.
04
Feels
The emotions behind it all: stress, joy, confusion. Note what triggers each one.
( Optional extras )

Many teams add two boxes below the map. Pains are fears, frustrations and obstacles. Gains are what the person hopes to achieve and how they'd measure success.

( Five steps )
01
Pick one person
Use a real user, or a persona based on research. Never map "everyone".
02
Gather evidence
Interview, observe, read survey answers. Write each finding on its own sticky note.
03
Sort the notes
Place each note in Says, Thinks, Does or Feels. Keep quotes in the person's own words.
04
Hunt for contradictions
Where Says and Does disagree, or Thinks and Feels clash, you've found your best clue.
05
Write one insight
Finish with "Maya needs ___ because ___". That sentence drives the design.

Empathy map for Maya, first-year student

Maya needs clear proof that her order worked, because she screenshots receipts out of fear that it didn't.

The contradiction: she says she wants speed, but she spends time collecting evidence. A confirmation screen with a pickup time would remove that fear.

( What is it )

An empathy map is a snapshot. A journey map is the film. The wavy line shows where the experience soars and where it sinks.

A user journey map lays the stages of one task from left to right. For each stage it records what the person does, thinks and feels. The dips in the emotion line are your priorities, and the last row turns the map into a plan.

( The framework )

Every journey map is a grid. One axis is time, the other is what you want to learn.

Across the top are the steps the person takes, in order, from start to finish. Down the side are the themes you analyse at every step: what they do, what questions they have, what makes them happy, what hurts, and what you could improve. Each box in the grid gets a sticky note.

A simple user journey map framework: making and sharing a video
01
Horizontal axis: steps through time
List every step in order. In this example: record, download, import, edit, export, upload and share.
02
Vertical axis: themes for analysis
Ask the same five questions at every step: actions, questions, happy moments, pain points and opportunities.

( Which tool when )
Empathy map
Use it to understand who your user is. Best at the start, after your first interviews. It gives you one clear picture of a mindset.
Journey map
Use it to see where things go wrong over time. Best when you have a product or process to improve. It shows what to fix first.

The empathy map explains why Maya feels anxious. The journey map shows when that anxiety peaks.

A map is a conversation starter. Keep updating it every time you learn something new from a real person.

( Try it yourself )
01 — Interview a classmate
Ask a friend how they get lunch on a busy day. Take notes for five minutes, then sort them into Says, Thinks, Does and Feels.
02 — Map your own morning
Draw five stages of getting to class, from waking up to sitting down. Add an emotion curve and circle the lowest point.
03 — Find the contradiction
Look at your empathy map for a gap between what someone says and what they do. Write one idea to close it.
( The big idea )

Design starts with listening. Replace "I think users want" with "I saw that Maya needs".

The next time an app frustrates you, notice which stage of the journey broke. You've just spotted a gap that someone didn't map.
